Document register and surface treatments

Text roles, the space between them and the rails that carry depth; surfaces composed from three axes; disclosure and content emphasis. The owner demonstrates the rules it expects its consumers to follow — including on its own key pages.

rule · surface axes

Attachment, elevation and material are chosen separately

A surface treatment is not one recipe. Attachment governs which corners are rounded. Elevation governs whether the reader should perceive the object as sitting above its backing. Material governs fill, blur, and the foreground that stays readable over it. They compose, and none silently determines another.

Population

Every live surface composed against the page field or a canvas.

Excluded

Diagram nodes and artifact-scaffold cards. They are rectangles, not surfaces, and they carry their own semantics.

example

A navigation drawer hanging from the top edge rounds only its free corners; its material is chosen separately. Whether it also casts a shadow is a separate choice, made for its role — its attachment settles its corners and says nothing about its depth.

counterexample

Giving that same drawer a full radius would round a joined edge and visually detach it from the surface it hangs from.

Same role and same context resolve to the same treatment. A different treatment must correspond to a named difference in role, context or approved profile — never to the pattern in which someone happened to implement it.

rule · attachment

Attachment settles corners and nothing else

An attached surface rounds only its free corners. Each plane below takes the page material and flush elevation, so only its corners differ, and the line it touches marks the edge it is joined to. Attachment changes no fill, blur, edge or shadow. Pills belong to interactive elements; the chip plane shows that value's corners only.

Blur — one value

Where a surface's material blurs, it blurs at 20px — the panels set on the page material here do. No second value serves the same role. Blur belongs to the material, not to every container: the disclosure takes the panel material, which does not blur, and this value does not reach it.

Navigation composed over another surface (14px) and small floating chips (8px) are separate roles and are unchanged.

Interaction attention

Hover these and Tab through them. Hover and keyboard focus share ONE treatment, because they mean the same thing — you are addressing this control. Their state logic stays independent: focus survives the pointer leaving, and a control that is both hovered and focused shows one edge, not two.

Hierarchy rail

The hierarchy rail says "this belongs inside the level above." Below h3 the type stops changing: every deeper heading uses the h4 treatment, and a deeper level is that same section nested one step further inside the rail — heading and content together, to any depth. The same rail carries structured text whose indentation is structure, such as the lines that belong beneath a label in an outline or a definition list, so the levels inside a preformatted passage need no second treatment; the block itself stands behind the magenta block rail. The hierarchy rail is assigned by that relationship, not to every preformatted block; spaces that only align columns are not levels; and inside an emphasis rail it sits one inset further in, never on the same edge.

Structured text

Where a preformatted passage's indentation is structure, each level is one more neutral hierarchy rail inside the same scroll box, and the whole block sits behind one magenta block rail. A blank source line keeps its slot, so a blank line belongs only between groups: a blank between a label and the lines beneath it splits one group in two. At a narrow width the block scrolls and says so; its lines and its text size stay as they are.

Set-apart passages

A quotation, a preformatted block and an authorial callout are set apart from the reading flow, each as one unit, on one rail geometry: 2px, with the text inset by the same step. Every one of those outer rails is an emphasis accent, so each reads as a boundary in both themes, and the color says whose passage it is. A quotation is represented voice — words presented as someone's — so its rail is violet. The document's own thesis, posed question or contrast is an authorial callout, so its rail is magenta. A block's line structure is its content, so its rail is magenta too. Neutral is structure only: the hierarchy rail that structures a section, a list or a block. Who is speaking is the consuming surface's assignment, never the element, the font or a pair of quotation marks.

A short statement presented as someone else's words is a quotation, on the violet rail.

Section synthesis

A synthesis states what the part before it comes to. It is larger in scope than a callout, so it is contained rather than railed, and every synthesis in a document takes the same composition whatever the status of its text. The chip names it; only the chip's border takes the accent, so the panel keeps its neutral perimeter and gains no bloom. The text stays at the body step, with its authored emphasis.

compression

A section synthesis is one flat panel: tinted, neutral at its edge, free at its corners, with body-sized text and no rail.

Dense tables

A dense table is declared, not inferred: the consumer marks it table.doc-dense-table, and then its cells are tabular payload, mono at the Small step with tabular numerals, and its headers take the operative label. Being a table does not make a table dense: a narrative comparison stays prose and waits for the shared table work.

Entry titles

An entry in a result list or record index opens one record. Its primary text is the entry title: Body's size, leading and tracking at 300, one weight step above the prose around the list, because it may be a short name or a full statement that wraps. It is not a heading and governs nothing beneath it, and it is not the panel's single-line label. The identifier and the supporting fields take the metadata role; the entry's element, interaction and row geometry stay the consumer's.

A labeled connector

A decorative rule can simply stop at its label. A line that asserts a relation carries meaning, so its remedy has to keep that meaning — its ends, its direction and its path. Here the connector runs unbroken and the label takes its own row above it: one way to keep the relation, not the only one.
