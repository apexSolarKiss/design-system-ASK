Review Semantics

One review vocabulary, composed with the document register, Evidence State and Spectral State. Each of five questions keeps its own answer: what a passage is doing, where it came from, what the evidence supports, what judgment is asked or made, and what condition an architectural element is in.

Five questions, five owners

QuestionAnswered byIt cannot establish
What is this passage doing?Document role: prose, quotation, callout, framing, synthesisWhether it is true, accepted or in force
Where did it come from?Basis: a keyed value, no colorWhether it is supported or accepted
What does the evidence support?Evidence State: a dotAcceptance, permission or maturity
What judgment is asked or made, about what, by whom, at which scope?Review State: an outlineA new origin, new evidence or authority beyond the decision
What condition is this architectural element in?Spectral State: a squareThat every claim or use around the element is accepted

Review State // the five roles

MarkMeaningValue and relationship
ProposedA candidate statement or change, awaiting a decision. It says nothing about whether the content is wrong or weakly supported.Spectral proposed, by reference: a candidate on another axis
Correction requestedThe same role, used only when someone has asked for existing words to change. It shares paint with Proposed, not event identity.As above
Open questionA named matter held until someone answers it.Spectral held, by reference: an element deliberately held open, on another axis. Evidence unresolved shares the value.
AcceptedA recorded decision to adopt or to permit, naming what, who and the scope. A narrow scope is a whole decision, not a partial one.Spectral earned, by reference: green
DeclinedA recorded decision not to adopt or not to permit, naming what, who and the scope. It does not say the statement is false.Spectral deflated, by reference: red
No decision recordedShown only where the absence matters. It is not a refusal or an acceptance, and not a hold: a hold is an open question.The faintest neutral

Rows and lists read in one order: state, basis, evidence, review. The same value on two axes is a correlation, not a synonym. Open question and Unresolved share a value because both name something not yet settled; their geometry and their words still say which axis is speaking.
