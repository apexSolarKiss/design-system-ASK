Review Semantics

One review vocabulary, composed with the document register, Evidence State and Spectral State. Each of five questions keeps its own answer: what a passage is doing, where it came from, what the evidence supports, what judgment is asked or made, and what condition an architectural element is in.

Five questions, five owners

QuestionAnswered byIt cannot establish
What is this passage doing?Document role: prose, quotation, callout, framing, synthesisWhether it is true, accepted or in force
Where did it come from?Basis: a keyed value, no colorWhether it is supported or accepted
What does the evidence support?Evidence State: a dotAcceptance, permission or maturity
What judgment is asked or made, about what, by whom, at which scope?Review State: an outlineA new origin, new evidence or authority beyond the decision
What condition is this architectural element in?Spectral State: a squareThat every claim or use around the element is accepted

Rows and lists read in one order: state, basis, evidence, review. A row that opens a range, leads a whole list item or stands as a prompt before a range leads with its review item, and its other items follow in that order. The same value on two axes is a correlation, not a synonym. Open question and Unresolved share a value because both name something not yet settled; their geometry and their words still say which axis is speaking.

The review mark and the chip share one box, the bordered label: the same padding, corner and edge. Its small corner keeps an inert mark from looking like a control beside a compact action. The mark differs from the chip in its fainter label words, its review vocabulary and its place, leading the range it opens (whose scope rail sits outside the content edge), the whole list item it judges or a prompt before a range, or sitting in the cell, note, list or document record that bounds its scope, not in its color: a Proposed mark and a thesis chip share the emphasis magenta. The chip names why a unit is emphasized and heads a panel.
