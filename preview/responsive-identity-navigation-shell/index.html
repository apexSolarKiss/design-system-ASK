A prototype of the responsive identity-navigation shell. The opening header is ordinary document content on both breakpoints; what differs is which mark persists and where its panel comes from.

Scroll. On a wide viewport the mark settles from 64px to 40px over the first 24px and then holds, and the breadcrumb and lede scroll away beneath it. Activate the mark to bring the navigation panel down.

On a narrow viewport nothing is pinned at the top. The opening mark and breadcrumb scroll away as ordinary document content, and a second mark enters from the lower right in phase with the first one's exit — both movements complete at the same instant.

The fade and the button travel as one object, so content is always cleared before it reaches the mark. Scroll back up and the handoff reverses exactly.

The probe in the corner reports the live progress value and where it comes from: the opening mark's own rect, not a percentage of the page. That distinction matters because the terminal reserve below changes the page's total height — a percentage-driven handoff would retime itself whenever the footer, the content length or the browser chrome changed.

Keep scrolling to the very bottom to check the terminal clearance: the footer should finish comfortably above the fade, with the mark fully seated and every footer link reachable.
