Criterion Assessment

How does this characteristic serve the stated criterion? Favorable, caution or adverse, against a criterion the author declares, or unassessed. The profile owns what each role means and how it is drawn; the author owns the criterion and every assignment.

Four roles, three colors

  • favorable // value of state-earned

  • caution // value of state-structural, reinforced on the lavender field

  • adverse // value of state-deflated

  • unassessed // no rail

RoleMeansNever means
Favorablesupports the row's criterionaccepted, supported, or good overall
Cautionconditional, mixed or intermediate against itdanger, or an unknown
Adverseworks against the row's criteriondeclined, weakened, or bad overall
Unassessedno judgment asserteda neutral rating or a warning

The three colors are Spectral State's, by reference, so they change with the theme as Spectral State does. On the light lavender field, caution's rail takes a 1px reinforcement, because Spectral State's yellow all but disappears there as a thin rail; in dark, or on another field a root declares, it keeps the bare stroke, and its color is unchanged. They are visual-role mappings, not aliases: a surface keeps the words Favorable, Caution and Adverse, and never writes earned, structural or deflated for them.

A worked comparison

Three ways to deliver a reviewed document, against four rows. Each assessed summary carries one rail in its cell's lane, beginning on the edge its column's header begins on, and the word that names it; the rail is as tall as the summary and its word, never the row. The last row declares no criterion, so its summaries carry no rail, and their text sits where the others' does.

An assessment beside a review mark

The PDF's fidelity cell carries two judgments, and each keeps its own form. Its caution rail says how the PDF serves the row's criterion. Its open question is a review mark from Review Semantics: its own bordered box, its own color and its own words. The mark does not make the caution a review decision, and the rail does not answer the question.

The same holds for an evidence mark. A favorable characteristic is not a supported claim, and a supported disadvantage is still adverse.

A legend-led comparison

The same three options, with a row of summaries and two rows of figures. The table is legend-led: its key names each rail, so the words stay in the cells as text for assistive technology and draw nothing, and each rail is as tall as its summary or figure. Each summary spans its family's columns, so its rail begins where the family's header does. The figures share a value field, as wide as the template 000.00 that their widest needs: each sits at its column's end, and in each column the rails share one line, 12px before the field, however wide the figure. A sighted reader reads these assessments from the rails' colors against the key, so the worked comparison above keeps its words.

One paint per rail color

How a color is painted as a rail belongs to the surface treatments, not to this profile: a rail in one color takes the same paint whichever meaning draws it. This profile binds each role to its color's paint, as Review Semantics binds a review range's. Here a declined range and an adverse assessment share one red, and an accepted range and a favorable assessment one green, each rail in its own place: a range's in the outer margin, an assessment's in its lane.

Caution's yellow is the one yellow drawn as a rail, and its ring is the same beside a summary, in a value field and in a key, above. A review range is never yellow.

Calibrated for the field

A rail is calibrated for the field it sits on, not for a light theme. Two layers do it: Spectral State sets each color's value for its theme's field, and the surface treatments add a dose where a color still fails there as a bare stroke. On the light lavender field the bare yellow all but disappears, so caution's rail takes a ring; green and red read as a bare stroke and take none. On the dark ink field every rail is bare. The dose changes paint only: the color, the rail's place, length and gap, the meaning and the text stay as they are. It is the surface treatments' one rail paint, so a review range in green or red takes the same none as favorable and adverse, and caution, the one yellow drawn as a rail, is the ring's only caller (One paint per rail color, above). A root that declares no field is taken to paint its theme's own: the lavender in light, the ink in dark. A root that declares one with data-surface-field keeps the ring only for ask-lavender in light; any other declaration keeps the bare stroke: ask-ink is the dark field's own, and an unreviewed field keeps it until a rail is measured on it. Not this calibration: a bordered label's edge reinforcement, which every label color takes per theme, a diagram's stroke, a dot or a square.
